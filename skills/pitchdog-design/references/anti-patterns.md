# Anti-patterns and repairs

## Reference and prestige traps

### Apple cosplay

**Signal:** giant centered type, strategic full stops, glass, monochrome chrome, copied product-page sequence.

**Failure:** recognizable surface without product truth, compression, causality, or restraint.

**Repair:** recover the useful truth; let one exact choice carry it.

### Awwwards as outcome

**Signal:** preloaders, custom cursors, long pins, WebGL, and spectacle designed for other designers.

**Failure:** direct retrieval, access, and conversion pay for theatre.

**Repair:** keep only interactions that change understanding or direct manipulation.

### Premium adjectives

**Signal:** “cinematic,” “luxury,” “bold,” “immersive,” or “premium” used as a specification.

**Failure:** adjective substitutes for audience effect and material decision.

**Repair:** name the intended change and the design move that causes it.

## Product and component traps

### Polished SaaS in pitch.dog colours

**Signal:** centered hero, pill eyebrow, rounded feature grid, logo ticker, testimonial carousel, gradient call to action.

**Failure:** generic information architecture survives a palette swap.

**Repair:** rebuild content units from the subject: proof, pages, frames, notes, rows, chapters, tools, or data.

### Anti-SaaS theatre

**Signal:** familiar controls removed so a tool can look editorial.

**Failure:** ideology about visual novelty makes work slower.

**Repair:** restore predictable operation; bring character through hierarchy, language, states, and finish.

### Card monoculture

**Signal:** every idea receives equal radius, border, padding, and shadow.

**Failure:** boundary replaces hierarchy.

**Repair:** ask what real object or action each boundary represents; remove unearned containers.

### Component-library art direction

**Signal:** pages assembled from modules before their arguments exist.

**Failure:** consistency replaces relevance.

**Repair:** design representative pages first; extract repeated foundation and behavior afterward.

## Material and motion traps

### Artisanal SaaS

**Signal:** generic startup wireframe plus serif, paper texture, tape, and grain.

**Failure:** craft surface hides generic structure.

**Repair:** remove the manufactured layer, run the silhouette test, and redesign the structure.

### Paper cosplay

**Signal:** random tears, stains, parchment, handwriting, photocopy damage, or rotation.

**Failure:** nostalgia impersonates tactility.

**Repair:** use real boundaries, overlap, crop, sequence, contact, and proof.

### Empty elegance

**Signal:** tiny copy stranded in huge fields or `100vh` used as padding.

**Failure:** vacancy impersonates calm.

**Repair:** make one meaningful object decisive; use proximity and chapter rhythm.

### Motion quota

**Signal:** every page gets sticky scenes, parallax, hover floats, stagger, and reveals.

**Failure:** time and fragility increase without new meaning.

**Repair:** define X, Y, and Z; remove motion with no causal Z.

### Fake fluidity

**Signal:** smooth scrolling, bounce, blur, or spring presets added without current-value interruption or touch testing.

**Failure:** motion looks lively but resists the user.

**Repair:** restore immediate response, direct tracking, spatial origin, reversal, and reduced motion—or make it static.

## Brand and system traps

### House-style takeover

**Signal:** client work receives pitch.dog type, paper, signals, cards, dogs, or page choreography.

**Failure:** studio recognizability erases client authorship.

**Repair:** switch to Mindset Mode and derive the surface from the target.

### Brand-kit cosplay

**Signal:** correct fonts, colours, grid, and timings on a weak argument.

**Failure:** token compliance impersonates brand quality.

**Repair:** fix job, narrative, proof, and hierarchy before adding more tokens.

### Same-answer system

**Signal:** repeated hero, blush/powder order, card stack, scroll story, joke, dog, or page count.

**Failure:** a describable system flattens subjects.

**Repair:** keep semantic foundations; require a subject-native thesis per artifact.

### Mascot wallpaper

**Signal:** dogs, paws, woofs, or Off-Leash language everywhere.

**Failure:** affection becomes noise and brand caricature.

**Repair:** use approved assets when they have a role; let the work lead.

## Completion traps

### Responsive stacking

**Signal:** desktop columns become one very long phone column.

**Failure:** order, density, and interaction remain desktop-shaped.

**Repair:** edit phone as its own edition.

### Checklist accessibility

**Signal:** an AA claim without keyboard, zoom, error, motion, or assistive-technology evidence.

**Failure:** compliance language hides unusable behavior.

**Repair:** test the artifact and report checked/unchecked surfaces exactly.

### Producer's “done”

**Signal:** files exist, tests pass, screenshot looks good, or URL resolves.

**Failure:** the real artifact, device, or audience remains untested.

**Repair:** inspect the real result and keep built, rendered, checked, installed, deployed, published, live, and accepted distinct.

### Overbuilding

**Signal:** a button review becomes a design system or a strong page gets rebuilt.

**Failure:** activity displaces outcome and destroys accepted work.

**Repair:** rank failures by consequence; make the smallest bounded repair.
