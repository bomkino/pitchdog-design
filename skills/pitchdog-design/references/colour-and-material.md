# Colour and material

Colour creates hierarchy, state, atmosphere, and respect for the work. It is not a rotating decoration schedule or a compliance badge.

In Mindset Mode, accepted target-brand colour and material authority wins. Use this document to fill only missing relationships and label those decisions inferred or provisional. Do not replace a legitimate external palette, gradient, corner, translucency, or shadow language with pitch.dog taste. Proof integrity and the no-additive-grain boundary remain universal.

## Use semantic roles

Define a small role set before choosing values:

- ground;
- surface and raised surface;
- primary and muted text;
- boundary;
- signal or accent;
- focus;
- success, warning, and danger;
- proof frame or project field.

For digital work, author every role for Light and Dark. Components consume roles, never raw palette values. For physical media, resolve roles through the production process rather than inventing interface themes. Keep sRGB as a complete digital baseline; use Display-P3 only as a progressive enhancement when its difference is visible and worth maintaining. Wide gamut is not a design outcome.

Set alpha in the colour itself rather than lowering the opacity of a parent and washing out its content. Keep disabled, muted, and decorative states distinguishable without making essential information faint.

## Colour as local climate

Stay quiet enough for client work and evidence to remain sovereign. A project may change the room when its colour is meaningful. Keep that colour local to its proof or chapter instead of turning every section into a new theme.

Use a signal colour for an actual signal: action, focus, status, annotation, or a decisive editorial turn. If removing a colour does not change meaning or hierarchy, it is optional decoration.

Do not tint, recolour, grade, or place overlays over approved project proof without authority. Check source colour, rights, and intended presentation.

## Brand Mode material logic

Build tactility from things that truly exist in the composition:

- hard or nearly hard edges;
- fine rules and visible boundaries;
- page edges, crop, overlap, occlusion, and contact;
- real handwriting, annotations, guide marks, corrections, or project residue;
- shadows only where one surface actually meets or lifts from another;
- contrast between controlled structure and one human interruption.

Never add grain, film grain, noise, SVG turbulence, distressed-paper overlays, or pseudo-texture. Preserve texture already intrinsic to approved source art; do not add or amplify it.

In Brand Mode, avoid gradient fog, translucent layer stacks, fake tape, random rotation, universal rounded cards, and soft shadows with no physical relationship. In Mindset Mode, judge those choices against accepted target-brand authority and the artifact's job.

## Verification

1. Grayscale: hierarchy survives colour removal.
2. Theme or production condition: digital work preserves hierarchy, proof, focus, and state in both authored modes; physical work is checked in its actual stock, ink, profile, lighting, and proof conditions.
3. Contrast: text, meaningful icons, controls, focus, and status meet the target.
4. Proof: surrounding colour does not alter or compete with owner-supplied work.
5. Output colour: digital work has a complete sRGB baseline and any P3 enhancement fails safely; physical work is checked in its chosen output profile and proof conditions.
6. Material: remove decorative surface effects. The composition still feels handled because its relationships are real.
