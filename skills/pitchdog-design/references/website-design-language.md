# Website design language

Use this branch for pitch.dog websites and closely related digital editorial work. It distils the durable grammar from the Website v12 research without turning one homepage into a template.

## Governing sentence

**Quiet field. Loud work. One strange little thing.**

The work is sovereign. The frame steps back. Give each authored editorial scene one primary event, at most one secondary whisper, and enough room for both to register. Dense operational surfaces use a clear task hierarchy instead of this scene cadence. Stop before the interface starts auditioning beside the subject.

## Build relationships, not templates

Useful recurring relationships include:

- large field / small event;
- proof / quiet metadata;
- hard edge / imperfect line;
- ordinary object / one impossible behaviour;
- controlled composition / one human interruption.

These are tensions, not component recipes. Chapters may use different grammars. Family resemblance comes from proportion, interval, typography, restraint, line, evidence, and judgment—not repeated hero structures, colours, card stacks, jokes, or scroll scenes.

## Sequence the page

1. Name the page’s one job and the audience change it must cause.
2. Map the proof that earns each important claim.
3. Find the subject’s engine: the tension, transformation, object, or behaviour only this page can own.
4. Give every chapter one job and one dominant silhouette.
5. Make an excellent authored still before motion.
6. Design phone as its own edit, not a collapsed desktop.
7. Delete until the remaining events have authority.

Empty space is active when it creates pressure, pace, anticipation, relief, or value. It fails when tiny content is stranded inside a fashionable viewport.

## Let proof own the room

Put real projects, pages, frames, products, people, data, and testimony before broad claims. Keep credits, roles, rights, and project facts exact. Preserve source aspect ratio and colour unless approved art direction says otherwise. Never manufacture portfolio proof to satisfy a layout.

Treat an illustration as a complete visual poem: preserve its field, negative space, scale relationships, and contextual marks. Do not extract characters into mascot stickers or spread dogs, paws, and jokes as wallpaper.

## Components are instruments

Choose the page job, then the driver, then the silhouette, then the reusable behaviour. A component may standardise accessibility, state, data, or interaction mechanics. It must not dictate the chapter’s argument.

Prefer static HTML, CSS, and SVG when they carry the idea. Use a framework, canvas, WebGL, or code component only when the required behaviour earns its cost and has a complete static/reduced-motion answer.

## Colour, type, and motion

Typography comes only from the resolved pitch.dog type system. Colour is a local climate with authored Light and Dark modes; it is not a rotating decoration schedule. Motion belongs to a semantic owner—proof, navigation, state, or transformation—and must complete, reverse, interrupt, and disappear under reduced motion without losing the story.

For material presence, obey [colour and material](colour-and-material.md): approved artwork and real compositional evidence must do the work.

## What remains project-specific

Do not promote a prototype’s exact sequence, named scenes, palette values, breakpoints, component names, sticky heights, illustration placement, code structure, or one-off interaction into global law. Those choices belong to their page until another real use proves a shared contract.

## Stop condition

The page holds when an unfamiliar person can explain what it is, real work supports the claims, one image remains in memory, phone and wide editions feel authored, both themes retain hierarchy, and deleting another element would damage meaning rather than merely reduce decoration.
