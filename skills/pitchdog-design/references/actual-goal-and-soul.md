## Audience outcome

Begin with what should become true because the artifact exists. A strong result lets the intended person:

- understand the subject without decoding design theatre;
- find proof close to the claim it supports;
- feel that somebody paid attention;
- trust the work and the people behind it;
- retain the central idea;
- act, decide, recover, or decline without coercion;
- and recognize the subject's own authorship.

“Designed like pitch.dog” is a constraint, not an outcome.

## Proxy-gap test

For consequential work, identify:

1. The real audience change.
2. The visible cue most likely to impersonate success.
3. The cheapest polished version that satisfies that cue while failing the job.
4. The harm caused by that false success.
5. Artifact evidence that separates the real result from imitation.
6. The smallest useful intervention.

Common proxies include “Apple-like,” “Awwwards-ready,” “premium,” “cinematic,” “tactile,” Display-P3, full bleed, spaciousness, cards, motion, responsiveness, and checklist accessibility.

Countercheck: remove the cue. If hierarchy, proof, task completion, and emotional meaning collapse, the cue was camouflage. Restore it only when it deepens the outcome.

## Human qualities

### Soul

Treat soul as specific attention with a point of view. Look for a crop that matters, an honest empty state, a protected quote, a useful margin note, a meaningful line break, a credit left visible, or an interaction arising from the subject. Texture cannot manufacture this.

### Warmth

Reduce needless labour. Use clear labels, humane errors, preserved work, generous reading, intact testimony, obvious next actions, and a route to say no. Warmth is not beige, rounded corners, exclamation marks, or forced friendliness.

### Love

Finish the parts that do not photograph well: alt text, legal terms, slow networks, ordinary phones, 200% zoom, dark mode, private states, credits, fallbacks, file names, and handover. Love is operational care.

### Wit

Find one semantic or formal turn native to the subject. Let it explain, lower anxiety, or make a benefit memorable. Stop after the turn. Do not stack a clever headline, clever button, clever tooltip, and clever error.

### Quiet confidence

Use enough. Give one thing authority, state facts, show proof, permit stillness, and end when the argument is complete. Do not claim “meticulous,” “world-class,” or “premium” when the artifact can demonstrate the standard.

### Craft

Make the object survive inspection: optical type, image treatment, states, responsive composition, interaction physics, keyboard focus, performance, source truth, and maintainable structure. Craft is evidence, not an adjective.

## Material politics

Apply politics where it changes conditions:

- make labour, scope, and payment legible;
- preserve authorship and collaborator credit;
- separate consent from pressure;
- keep private material private;
- avoid unpaid homework disguised as onboarding;
- design accessible paths as core paths;
- support undo and recovery;
- avoid dark patterns, scarcity theatre, and status coercion;
- make ownership, deletion, and data use understandable.

Do not decorate unrelated work with ideological slogans. Change the arrangement of power, time, risk, and credit.

## Protected truth

Never change accepted copy, facts, names, pronouns, titles, quotes, numbers, URLs, credit, permissions, legal/commercial terms, or owner-approved decisions without explicit authority.

Keep unknown fonts, proof, status, permissions, or facts unknown. A visible gap is safer than plausible fiction.

## Proportional pre-design brief

Recover only what changes the work:

- audience and context;
- desired understanding, feeling, action, or decision;
- available content and proof;
- central tension or useful truth;
- protected material;
- active brand profile;
- medium and native constraints;
- costliest polished failure;
- evidence required for completion.

For a web page, also name its one job, page-specific tension, proof map, dominant visual idea, next route, and whether motion changes understanding.

Ask at most one strategic question when no responsible default exists. Small artifact, small intervention.
